# Editor note: the lantern / light image

Repeated image: a lantern, and the light it gives (the poems also share a dark or cold surround).

## Where it appears
- poems/harbor-lantern.md, line 3: "A lantern swings above the pier,"
- poems/harbor-lantern.md, line 4: "its light a slow and patient tide."
- poems/winter-bread.md, line 4: "a lantern in the kitchen's corner."
- poems/paper-moths.md, line 6: "All of them found the light."

(Line numbers count the title as line 1 and the blank line as line 2.)

## Observations
- The image does different work each time: guidance and listening (harbor), domestic warmth against frost (bread), pull toward something unreachable (moths). That variety is a strength.
- The literal word "lantern" is used in two poems. In "Winter Bread" it is the only lantern-like element and sits as a bare statement, while "Harbor Lantern" already owns the lantern as its title and subject.
- "light" in "Paper Moths" works best because it is unadorned and unexplained. It is the strongest echo of the lantern without repeating the word.

## Suggestions (no new lines proposed)
1. Winter Bread, line 4: consider whether the lantern is needed. Cutting the line would keep the poem's quiet domestic focus (flour, dough, frost) and avoid duplicating Harbor Lantern's object. If kept, it is the one line that feels placed rather than found.
2. Harbor Lantern, line 4: "slow and patient tide" stacks two soft adjectives. Consider cutting one of them, leaning on the tide metaphor alone.
3. Paper Moths, line 6: leave as is. If the lantern repeats elsewhere, this is where the reader should feel it arrive, so avoid adding a second lantern word here.
4. Across the set: decide whether the lantern is a deliberate motif. If yes, keep it in Harbor Lantern and Paper Moths (as "the light") and let Winter Bread stand without it. If no, the Winter Bread line is the one to change.
